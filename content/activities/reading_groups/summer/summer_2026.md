## Elliptic Curve and Cryptography

This introductory reading group develops the basics of elliptic curves and discusses applications in number theory and cryptography.

The first part focuses on foundational theory: algebro-geometric foundations, group law, basic arithmetic properties, and some finiteness and integrality results.

The second part focuses on applications, especially elliptic curve cryptography, factoring, and selected conjectures with their implications.

- **References:** No strict choices but some options are:
  - _The Arithmetic of Elliptic Curves_ -- J. H. Silverman
  - _Rational Points on Elliptic Curves_ -- J. H. Silverman and J. Tate
  - _Introduction to Elliptic Curves and Modular Forms_ -- N. Koblitz
  - _Elliptic Curves: Number Theory and Cryptography_ -- L. C. Washington
- **Pre-requisites:** Basic understanding of abstract algebra, number theory, complex analysis, and some algebraic curves. More algebraic geometry, basic cryptography, and Galois theory can be developed along the way.

## Algebraic Geometry

This summer reading project in algebraic geometry aims to present the classical theory of varieties over algebraically closed fields.

The first goal is to develop enough theory to prove birational classification results for nonsingular curves, including classification of rational curves up to isomorphism, a proof of Luroth's theorem, and a proof of the Zariski cancellation problem for curves.

A second goal is to cover a generalized form of Bezout's theorem and an introduction to arithmetic genus.

- **Pre-requisites:** Some prerequisites in commutative algebra were expected, with Atiyah-Macdonald material up to dimension theory recommended.
- **Primary References:** No strict choices but some options are:
  - _Algebraic Geometry_ -- Robin Hartshorne (Chapter I)
  - _Commutative Algebra_ -- Matsumura (for background)
- **Group Leader:**
  - Sourish Goswami (Research Scholar)

## Random Matrix Theory

This reading group aims to cover the following topics in random matrix theory.

- **Topics:**
  1. **Spectral distributions for GOE and GUE matrices:** Unlike most matrix ensembles, the point process of eigenvalues of GOE and GUE ensembles has a determinantal structure. We will derive this and explore some consequences from [AGZ10].
  2. **Semicircle law for Wigner matrices:** We will discuss the Stieltjes Transform method, an effective analytic tool which allows us to analyze spectral properties of Hermitian random matrices. These topics will be covered from [Tao12] and [AGZ10].
  3. **Circular law:** Conjectured back in the 1960s, the proof of the circular law remains one of the crowning achievements of the 2010s. We will discuss Girko's hermitization technique and the log-potential, alongside bounds on singular values to guarantee integrability of log-potential. We will follow [BC12].
